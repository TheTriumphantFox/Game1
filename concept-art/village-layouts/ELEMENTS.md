# Every element in the concept art

An inventory of `01-village-of-the-lost.png` (the forest village, 1254x1254),
read at 3x magnification across six crops: a north house pair, the fountain
plaza, the north-west tree border and rock wall, the north gate, an open grass
verge, and one diagonal inner-ring house.

Cross-checked against `04-frostfast-hold.png` to separate what is structural
(true in all 13) from what is forest dressing. Those two images share their
geometry exactly and differ only in palette and prop set, which is the single
most useful fact in this document: **one set of shapes, thirteen skins.**

## 9. Light and rendering rules

These are not objects, they are the constraints that make the picture cohere.
Any redraw that breaks one of them will look wrong however good the individual
sprites are.

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| 54 | One light direction, upper-left. Every shadow in the image falls down and to the right. |
| 55 | Every raised object casts a soft shadow, and shadow length scales with the object's height. Bush short, house medium, fountain long. |
| 56 | Every shape carries a near-black ink outline of consistent weight. |
| 57 | Fills are flat-ish: 2 to 3 discrete tone steps per shape rather than a smooth gradient. |
| 58 | Soft global vignette toward the corners. |
| 59 | **No tile seam is visible anywhere in the image.** Not in the grass, not in the treeline, not in the paving. The only regular grid is the plaza's flagstones, which are deliberate masonry. |

## What the art is not

It is worth being blunt about this, because it shapes what a faithful redraw can
and cannot deliver.

**The concept art is not 2.5D.** It is orthographic top-down, beautifully
rendered. Items 34 (no trunks) and 24 (the fountain columns are the sole
exception) are the proof. Drawing exactly what is in these images produces a
gorgeous top-down village, not a village with height.

So the art and the 2.5D goal are pulling in slightly different directions, and
they have to be reconciled deliberately rather than by accident. The reconciliation:

- Take the **palette, texture, ink language and detail density** from the art.
  That is items 1 to 53, and it is where the current procedural tiles are weakest.
- Take the **height** from somewhere the art does not supply it: objects anchored
  at their foot and drawn upward past their own cell, with a ground shadow at the
  foot and depth sorting against actors. The codebase already does exactly this
  for `drawColossalTree` and `drawBigLandmark`.

Item 59 is the one that argues hardest for getting art off the tile box. Nothing
in this picture is 48 pixels wide.

# Region 3: Tideborn Refuge

`03-tideborn-refuge.png`, the coast (region id `water` in regions.js).

**This is the first sketch that does not simply re-skin the forest**, and the
claim two sections up that all thirteen share their geometry exactly is too
strong. What is shared is the SKELETON: four cardinal roads, a ring, sixteen
houses in the same arrangement, a central plaza with a fountain. What is not
shared is everything between the roads. The forest and desert fill those
quadrants with open ground; the coast fills them with tidepools, so most of the
village is water and the houses sit on sand bars reached by plank jetties.

The border changes in kind too. The forest and desert are bounded by growth you
can stand next to; the coast is bounded by open ocean behind a rock breakwater,
which is ground rather than props.

## The border is deliberately NOT re-skinned

`T.MOUNTAIN` keeps its existing procedural art, alone among the five regions'
borders. Its art reads its eight neighbours to cap the crests that face open
ground, which is what makes a run of them look like a cliff face rather than a
row of rocks, and the per-cell prop system cannot express that. Replacing it
would have traded a mountainside for a boulder field.

The cost of that choice is measurable: earth is the slowest of the five regions
because most of an earth map is exactly that uncached, neighbour-dependent tile.
See the plan's Known gaps for the way out, which is real and not difficult.

**This region introduced no new prop shape at all.** Obsidian is the ice
region's faceted spire with a basalt body, the sulfur bushes are the forest's
shrub on an ochre ramp, and the ember flowers are the forest's drift on an
orange one. That is what the role split was for.

## New elements

| # | Element | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Lava flows | Bright molten channels outside the caldera rim. Dark crust broken by glowing cracks, which is the opposite construction to water: water is a bright field with dark bands, lava is a dark field with the glow showing through. |
| V2 | Magma cracks | Thin glowing seams running through the ash inside the village. |
| V3 | Obsidian spires | Black faceted crystals, the region's vertical object. |
| V4 | Sulfur shrubs | Ochre-yellow rosettes. |
| V5 | Ember flowers | Small orange blooms on charred stems. |
| V6 | Caldera rim | A dense band of black basalt boulders in place of a treeline. |

## The animated-tile problem, solved here

Lava and magma cracks both animate, and their pulsing glow is the whole reason
the region reads as volcanic. Leaving them out of the bake kept the motion but
gave every flow hard square tile edges next to ground whose every boundary is a
rounded region. Baking them killed the glow.

The answer is both: the tile goes in the ground table AND in the overlay set, so
the bake supplies the rounded shape and the static crust while the tile draws
its moving layer on top with its own base square suppressed. Water should be
converted the same way; see the plan.

**The hardest separation problem of the seven.** Cloud floor, cloud bank,
cobblestone and marble ALL sampled as the same near-white, roughly `#d9` to
`#f4`. Reproducing that literally collapsed the roads, the plaza and the ground
into one white field with the roads visible only as ghost outlines. The four are
pulled apart into a deliberate value ladder, roughly double the sketch's own
separation.

**And the ladder has to be set at ramp step 4, not across the whole ramp.**
`groundBase` fills with `lib.ramp(P, 0.65)`, which for a five-entry ramp
resolves to index 4 exactly, and the patch pass at 0.85 lands on 4 or 5. Step 4
is the tone a material actually reads as. Reading step 4 across the four pale
materials here: pave `#a8b0bd`, cloud `#cdd6e4`, plaza `#dee1e6`, cloudbank
`#eef1f7`. That is the ladder, and it is the generalised version of the note
recorded against the earth region.

## What this region cost the renderer

Cloud banks are open floor over about a third of the map, which exposed the last
hard-edged square boundary in the renderer: the join between the two ground
tones was painted per cell rather than traced. Tracing it fixed the look and
doubled the bake, which started dropping frames while walking. It is now opt-in
per region, and the chunk size came down from 768 px to 384 px so that each bake
is smaller. See the plan's walking table.

`09-solarspire-sanctum.png`, the luminous region (region id `luminous`).

The forest's layout on a golden floor, walled in by glowing white-gold crystal.

## Palette

| Crystal wall | `#d8bf72` `#e8d48c` `#f4e6b0` `#fbf3d4` `#fdfcf2` |
| Prismatic shards | `#b8cfe0` `#cce6f4` `#dceef8` `#ecf6fb` `#fbfcf9` |

| Plaza | `#cfc0aa` `#ddd0be` `#e6dbcb` `#ede2d0` `#f5eee4` |

**Paving is PALER than the ground here**, which is the reverse of every other
region and is what the sketch shows: cream roads across a golden field.

## Every tile in this region animates

Floor, glow pools, blooms, crystal border and light pillar all pulse. It is the
only region where that is true of everything, and it had two consequences.

**Visually it is the fullest use of bake-plus-overlay anywhere.** All of them
draw their light as translucent washes over the shared base square, which is
exactly what that path wants. Floor, glow pools and blooms are in the ground
table AND the overlay set, so the bake supplies a textured golden floor with
rounded paved regions and each tile keeps every bit of its shimmer. Only the
crystal border was taken as a prop, trading its pulse for a shape, because as
flat tiles that wall was just a coloured band and it is the region's signature.

**In performance terms it was the worst region in the game and nobody knew.**
Animated tiles cannot enter the sprite cache, so every one was redrawn per tile
per frame with trigonometry and several arcs. At `TILE_PX` 24 that measured
61.9 ms per frame against a 16.7 ms budget, about 16 fps. Baking the ground
under the animation rather than replacing it takes it to 4.3 ms.

## The crypt wall gave up its pulse

`T.BLIGHTED_WALL` animates, and the first pass kept that by putting it on the
bake-plus-overlay path. It looked wrong: as a tile it paints flat saturated
purple squares, and against the baked blight those read as a checkerboard rather
than as a wall. It is a PROP now, drawn as pale stone rubble, which is the same
trade the luminous region's crystal wall got and for the same reason.

## What this region cost, and what it was already costing

At `TILE_PX` 24 the necrotic overworld measured about **48 ms per frame** before
this work, against a 16.7 ms budget: the second worst region in the game, after
luminous. It is now 2.0 ms. Note that `oblique-conversion-plan.md` records this
same region at 7.56 ms and calls it the hottest map measured; the two figures
are not reconciled and the older one should not be relied on.
