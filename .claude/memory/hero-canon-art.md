`Game1/hero-portrait.png` (1024x1536 painted illustration, used directly by the
stats panel via `stats.js`) is the authoritative design for the player character:
a female elf knight in dark weathered steel (~`#636761`, not a bright
`#b9bfca`), heavy forest-green cloak, long swept-back ears, a large verdigris
tree-of-life shield as her signature element, and the sword carried high rather
than at the hip.

**Why:** any new player art should be built from the portrait, not by porting
`drawPlayer`'s geometry. That geometry is tuned for a 48px tile and its head and
hair proportions bury the armour when rendered as a standalone sprite.

**How to apply:** when asked for player art, read the portrait first.
`Game1/tools/make-hero-sheet.lua` generates `hero-sheet.png` plus
`hero-atlas.js`, and `hero-sprite.js` blits it from inside `drawPlayer`. The
procedural body in `drawPlayer` is now only the fallback for when the PNG fails
to load, so its divergences from the portrait matter less than they did.

Current sheet (redrawn at higher resolution 2026-08-26, commit aae51db): 128px
frames, 48px body box, 26 sheet columns, 52 frames per direction, 4 direction
rows. Animations are idle, walk, punch, jump, swim, climb, idle_armed,
walk_armed, jump_armed, idle_bow, walk_bow, jump_bow, bow, sword. The frame
layout is **not** duplicated by hand any more: `hero-atlas.js` is generated by
the Lua and loaded as a plain global (a `.js` assignment rather than the `.json`
atlas, because the game runs from `file://` where `fetch` cannot read a sibling
file). `hero-sprite.js` reads `HERO_ATLAS` with a hardcoded fallback for every
field, so a missing atlas degrades instead of throwing. Regenerate the atlas and
the sheet together; never hand-edit `hero-atlas.js`.
